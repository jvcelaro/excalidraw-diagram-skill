# Editorial brief

Write this brief before drawing. It is a compact decision record for the scene, not a second description of the visual style and not necessarily content to place on the canvas. Keep each field short enough to guide choices while building.

## Required fields

- **Audience:** Who must understand this, and what do they already know?
- **Question:** What single question should the diagram answer?
- **Takeaway:** What should the viewer be able to say or do after reading it? Write one sentence.
- **Scope / exclusions:** What is included, and what is deliberately left out so the scene stays teachable?
- **Format:** Which dominant format answers the question: mechanism, architecture, workflow with gates, comparison, evolution/timeline, decision, cheat sheet/taxonomy, concept map, or pattern board?
- **Reading direction:** Where does the eye start, and what is the intended order or branching path?
- **Entities:** Which actors, systems, artifacts, processes, decisions, boundaries, states, risks, and signals must be visible?
- **Evidence:** Which concrete payloads, events, fields, commands, metrics, examples, or observations make the claim credible?
- **Sources / inferences:** Which claims are directly sourced, and which are inferences or simplifications made for teaching?

## Compact template

```text
Audience:
Question:
Takeaway:
Scope / exclusions:
Format:
Reading direction:
Entities:
Evidence:
Sources / inferences:
```

Use the brief to reject attractive but irrelevant detail. If the entities or evidence cannot fit the chosen format without shrinking the text or multiplying containers, narrow the scope or split the story into an overview and a detail scene. Keep sources attached to the relevant evidence or note; do not present an inference as if it were a sourced fact.
